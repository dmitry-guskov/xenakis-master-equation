What Xenakis drew

Four pieces from the 1950s, each one an attempt to compose a distribution instead of a note. The master equation is the object all four are approximating.

Xenakis came to music through a drawing board. He trained as a civil engineer, spent a decade in Le Corbusier's studio, and the first thing he did to composition was to draw it. Before Metastaseis (1953–54) was a score for sixty-one players it was a sheet of graph paper: time along one axis, pitch up the other, and one straight line for each of the forty-six string players, each with a part of its own. In that plane a glissando at constant speed is a straight line, and its slope is a velocity in semitones per second. The notation came afterwards, as a transcription.

This is a stronger move than it looks. Take a family of straight lines and let their endpoints slide along two fixed edges: the lines stay straight, but the surface they sweep is curved — a ruled surface. The glissando masses that open and close Metastaseis are that construction heard rather than seen, and the Philips Pavilion Xenakis designed for the 1958 Brussels exposition is the same geometry poured in concrete. What matters musically is the consequence: no individual line is audible. Forty-six independent glissandi are not counterpoint. The ear keeps the envelope and the density and throws the parts away.

He had already made the argument in print. In The crisis of serial music (1955) he observes that once enough independent parts are superposed, the polyphony is not perceived as polyphony at all — what arrives is a statistical impression of the whole, and the elaborate row-work underneath is inaudible in principle, not merely in practice. The usual conclusion would be to write fewer parts. His was the opposite: if the ear hears statistics, then compose the statistics directly and let the parts be a sample drawn from them. From that point the score sits downstream of a probability law, and the compositional question becomes which law.

Pithoprakta (1955–56) — “actions through probability” — answers with the kinetic theory of gases, taken literally rather than as a figure of speech. Each of the forty-six strings is a molecule; its glissando has a velocity; the velocities are drawn from a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution. A passage is then specified by a temperature, and what the audience hears is a pitch gas expanding. Achorripsis (1956–57) moves the same idea from velocity to density: the plane of time against instrument is cut into cells and a Poisson law decides how many events land in each. What is composed is a rate.

Then, in Analogique A and B (1958–59), the piece that matters most for what follows, because it adds memory. Xenakis builds a book of screens — cells in a space of frequency, intensity and density — and moves from screen to screen with a transition matrix. That is a Markov chain over textures, in discrete time. It is the first object in the catalogue that is a stochastic process rather than a distribution resampled from scratch at every moment. Formalized Music collects all of it.

What is missing is a generator. Every one of these devices is built by hand and holds for one stretch of one piece: a velocity distribution here, a transition matrix there, tabulated in advance. Nowhere is there a single local rule — valid at every instant, on every chord — from which the distributions follow as consequences rather than being supplied as inputs. That object is standard equipment in statistical mechanics. Writing it down for Xenakis's material is what the rest of this page does.

Once it is written, the pieces come back as regimes of one equation rather than as four separate constructions: the free cloud of Pithoprakta, the ruled fan of Metastaseis, and the screen chain of Analogique — the last being what the process becomes when you sample it on a coarse lattice in discrete time. And it arrives with structure a hand-built scheme cannot have: probability is conserved by construction, there is a stationary distribution to converge to, and the temperature obeys the Einstein relation, so “hotter” means something checkable rather than metaphorical.

Two departures from Xenakis are deliberate and worth flagging now. Pitch here is quantised to the semitone, where his glissandi are continuous — the continuum is recovered in the limit below, and quantising is what makes the lattice, and later the quantum lift, available at all. More substantially, harmony enters through a potential on interval classes. Xenakis's stochastics are pointedly indifferent to consonance; that indifference was the point. Putting a tension operator in the exponent is the one thing here he would not have written, and it is what lets a single equation interpolate between a pitch gas and functional harmony.

00

The equation

A sound graph is a picture of pitch against time. The simplest object that generates one is a continuous-time random walk on pitch, with a bias.

Take three voices. A chord is a point \(n=(n_1,n_2,n_3)\) on the lattice \(\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{12}^{\,3}\) — twelve pitch classes, one coordinate per voice, 1728 points, a discrete 3-torus. The only elementary event is one voice sliding one semitone: \(n \to n \pm e_i\). Everything else is bookkeeping.

\[ \frac{\partial P(n,t)}{\partial t}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ \sum_{s=\pm 1}\Big[\,w_i^{s}(n-se_i)\,P(n-se_i,t)\;-\;w_i^{s}(n)\,P(n,t)\,\Big] \]
(M)

Gain minus loss, nothing more. What is being tracked matters, though, and it is easy to read it as the wrong object. \(P(n,t)\) is not a piece of music. It is the probability that the trio is on chord \(n\) at time \(t\), over every performance the rule could produce — Xenakis's mass, written exactly. A piece is a single sample path through the same rates: sit on a chord, wait an exponential holding time, jump. The equation propagates the cloud; sampling draws one line out of it. Both appear in the figure below — the cloud as the faint bundle, one path as the heavy line.

The music is entirely in the rates:

\[ w_i^{s}(n)\;=\;\frac{\gamma\,(1+s\,a_i)}{1+\exp\!\big(\beta\,[\,V(n+se_i)-V(n)\,]\big)} \]
(R)

Three knobs, each of which is a compositional decision:

  • \(\gamma\) — tempo: events per unit time per voice. It multiplies every rate, so it multiplies the generator as a whole, and changing it rescales time and does nothing else. It is the one parameter with no effect on what the music is, only on how fast it happens.
  • \(a_i\in(-1,1)\) — the glissando: a signed drift for voice \(i\), Xenakis's velocity, the slope of a line in the sound graph. On a circle a drift cannot displace a mean, because there is nowhere to displace it to; what it produces instead is a steady current, a rate of winding around the octave. That is the honest formalisation of a glissando — not a displacement, a persistent flow.
  • \(\beta\) — inverse temperature: how strongly harmony is enforced. At \(\beta=0\) every rate is \(\gamma/2\), the walk is free, and the stationary distribution is uniform over all 1728 chords — a rather exact statement of one thing “atonal” can mean. As \(\beta\to\infty\) only non-increasing moves survive, and the trio slides downhill and freezes into the ground manifold: 72 states, the twelve major triads in six voicings each. Everything audible happens in between.

The shape of (R) is a logistic in the tension difference, \(w=\gamma(1+s\,a_i)\,\sigma(-\beta\Delta V)\) with \(\Delta V=V(n+se_i)-V(n)\), and three properties follow from that directly. A move that leaves the tension unchanged happens at rate \(\gamma/2\) in either direction — a fair coin. A move downhill saturates at \(\gamma\): the trio cannot resolve a dissonance faster than the tempo allows, however badly it wants to. A move uphill is suppressed by \(e^{-\beta\Delta V}\) but never forbidden, so the chain stays irreducible and has a unique stationary distribution. No chord is unreachable; the ugly ones are merely rare, and at finite \(\beta\) the music will always eventually wander somewhere it should not.

Metropolis rates, \(\min(1,e^{-\beta\Delta V})\), would give the same stationary distribution with a different kinetics. Glauber is the choice here because it is smooth and bounded, which is what makes the continuum limit below come out clean.

These are Glauber (heat-bath) rates, which matters twice. They are bounded by \(\gamma(1+|a_i|)\), so the process never runs away; and at \(a=0\) they satisfy detailed balance exactly, since \(\big(1+e^{-\beta\Delta V}\big)/\big(1+e^{\beta\Delta V}\big)=e^{-\beta\Delta V}\). The stationary state is then Boltzmann, \(P_\infty(n)\propto e^{-\beta V(n)}\) — checked to \(10^{-18}\) below. Turning on a drift multiplies that ratio by \((1+a)/(1-a)\): detailed balance breaks, and a steady probability current runs around the pitch circle. That current is the glissando.

Rather than describe the regimes, here they are. Every line below is generated by (R) as you watch, by exact Gillespie sampling — the same rule as the equation, one realisation at a time.

The tension operator

\(V\) is where music theory enters, and it is a three-body potential:

\[ V(n)\;=\;-\lambda\!\!\sum_{i<j}c\big(n_i-n_j\big)\;+\;U\!\!\sum_{i<j}\delta_{n_i n_j}\;-\;\mu\,\max_{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{12}}\ \sum_{p\,\in\,\mathrm{set}(n)} h(p-r) \]

The first term is pairwise consonance: \(c(d)=1/\sqrt{pq}\) for the simplest 5-limit ratio \(p/q\) naming interval class \(d\), symmetrised over inversion, so the fifth and the fourth share \(3/2\) and \(c(7)=c(5)=0.408\) is the largest. The second penalises unisons — it is exactly the on-site repulsion \(U\) of the Bose–Hubbard model that appears in §03. The third is virtual pitch: how well the chord's pitch-class set matches the harmonic series over its best root, with \(h\) the series' weight \(1/k\) landing on pitch classes \(0,0,7,0,4,7,10,0\) for harmonics \(1..8\).

The third term is not decoration. With \(\mu=0\), pairwise consonance alone is minimised by a stack of fifths, not by a triad — two perfect fifths beat a fifth plus two thirds. The triad is not a two-body ground state. Switching on the three-body root-support term at \(\mu=\lambda\) reorders the spectrum so that the major triads sit at the bottom, and the quintal stack becomes the first excited band.

What it becomes in the continuum

Expanding (R) for slowly varying \(V\) — \(1/(1+e^{\beta V'})\approx \tfrac12-\tfrac{\beta}{4}V'\) — and keeping the first two Kramers–Moyal coefficients gives a Smoluchowski equation, one per voice:

\[ \partial_t P=-\sum_i \partial_i\Big[\Big(\gamma a_i-\tfrac{\gamma\beta}{2}\,\partial_i V\Big)P\Big]+\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_i \partial_i^2 P \]

with mobility \(\gamma\beta/2\) and diffusion \(D=\gamma/2\), so \(D/\text{mobility}=1/\beta\): the Einstein relation holds and \(\beta\) is a temperature in the honest sense. Set \(V=0\) and you get exactly the picture Xenakis drew for Pithoprakta — a cloud of pitch spreading as \(\sqrt{\gamma t}\), with each voice's glissando velocity \(a_i\) drawn from a distribution. Drawing those \(a_i\) from a Maxwell–Boltzmann law recovers his construction verbatim; equation (M) is the generator that construction was a sampling of. Keeping \(V\) turns the free cloud into motion on a harmonic landscape, and taking the process on a discrete lattice in discrete time is the Analogique A/B screen chain.

Plotted over chord space

The state space is a 12×12×12 torus. Plotting \(V\) on it turns harmony into a landscape, and the equation into motion on that landscape.

Each axis is one voice's pitch class, and opposite faces are identified — moving a voice up twelve semitones returns you to the same chord. Consonant chords are the deep points of the landscape, and the walk (M) is biased downhill by \(\beta\) and pushed sideways by the drifts \(a_i\).

02

In Baez–Biamonte form

Rewriting (M) as \(\dot\psi = H\psi\) on a stochastic Fock space, with \(H\) built from creation and annihilation operators over a Petri net.

Baez and Biamonte's move in Quantum Techniques for Stochastic Mechanics is to put probability distributions where quantum states normally go. A stochastic state is \(\psi=\sum_n P(n)\,|n\rangle\) with \(P(n)\ge0\), and normalisation is the pairing with the sum covector \(\langle-|=\sum_n\langle n|\), so \(\langle-|\psi\rangle=1\). Then (M) is

\[ \frac{d\psi}{dt}=H\psi,\qquad H\ \text{infinitesimal stochastic:}\quad H_{mn}\ge0\ (m\neq n),\qquad \langle-|H=0. \]

Time evolution is \(e^{tH}\), a stochastic map, in place of the unitary \(e^{-itH}\). To build \(H\) we need a Petri net.

The Petri net of a chord

Species: pairs \((i,p)\) — "voice \(i\) is on pitch \(p\)" — so \(3\times12=36\) species. A token in species \((i,p)\) is one instrument of section \(i\) sounding \(p\). Transitions: one per triple \(\tau=(i,p,s)\), consuming a token of \((i,p)\) and producing one of \((i,p+s)\) at rate \(r_\tau=w_i^s\). Every transition is unary — one token in, one token out — which is exactly why the resulting chain conserves the number of voices.

With the stochastic conventions \(a^\dagger|n\rangle=|n+1\rangle\) and \(a|n\rangle=n\,|n-1\rangle\) (multiplication by \(z\) and \(\partial_z\) on generating functions), the Baez–Biamonte Hamiltonian of a Petri net is

\[ H=\sum_{\tau}r_\tau\Big(a^{\dagger\,T(\tau)}-a^{\dagger\,S(\tau)}\Big)\,a^{S(\tau)} \]
(BB)

where \(S(\tau),T(\tau)\) are the source and target multisets and \(a^{\dagger m}=\prod_k (a^\dagger_k)^{m_k}\). For our net \(S(\tau)=e_{(i,p)}\) and \(T(\tau)=e_{(i,p+s)}\), so it collapses to a sum of single hops:

\[ \boxed{\;H=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\ \sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}_{12}}\ \sum_{s=\pm1} w_i^{s}(p)\ \Big(a^\dagger_{(i,\,p+s)}-a^\dagger_{(i,\,p)}\Big)\,a_{(i,\,p)}\;} \]

Probability conservation is now a one-line identity rather than a computation: \(\langle-|a^\dagger_k=\langle-|\) for every \(k\), because \(a^\dagger\) merely relabels basis vectors and \(\langle-|\) sums over all of them. Hence \(\langle-|\big(a^{\dagger T}-a^{\dagger S}\big)a^{S}=\langle-|a^S-\langle-|a^S=0\), so \(\langle-|H=0\) term by term.

Restricting to the sector with exactly one token per voice, \(\hat N_i=\sum_p a^\dagger_{(i,p)}a_{(i,p)}=1\), the operator \(a^\dagger_{(i,q)}a_{(i,p)}\) acts as \(|q\rangle\langle p|\) on voice \(i\), and (BB) reduces to the generator of (M) on the 1728-dimensional chord space. Verified to machine zero in two independent ways below.

The orchestral limit

The same net has a second equation attached to it — Baez and Biamonte's rate equation, the deterministic large-number limit, where \(x_{(i,p)}\) is the number of players in section \(i\) on pitch \(p\):

\[ \frac{dx_{(i,p)}}{dt}=\sum_\tau r_\tau\big(T(\tau)-S(\tau)\big)_{(i,p)}\ x^{S(\tau)} =\sum_{s=\pm1}\Big[w_i^{s}(p-s)\,x_{(i,p-s)}-w_i^{s}(p)\,x_{(i,p)}\Big] \]

Because every transition is unary, \(x^{S(\tau)}\) is linear and the rate equation has the same shape as the master equation. That is the precise sense in which Xenakis's masses behave like probabilities: for a unary net, one voice's distribution and a hundred voices' density obey the same equation. Add a genuinely bimolecular transition — a rule that fires only when two voices meet, say a real voice-leading constraint — and the two equations part company, with the mass acquiring nonlinear dynamics the single voice never had.

03

Quantum generalisation

Same Fock space, same Petri net, but amplitudes instead of probabilities: the chord becomes a superposition, and the score is a measurement record.

The Baez–Biamonte form makes the lift almost mechanical, because the only thing separating the two mechanics is which operators are allowed. Read the same species and transitions with bosonic \(b,b^\dagger\) and a self-adjoint generator, and the pitch circle becomes a lattice model:

\[ \hat H=-J\sum_{i,p}\Big(b^\dagger_{(i,p+1)}b_{(i,p)}+\text{h.c.}\Big)+\frac{U}{2}\sum_{i,p}\hat n_{(i,p)}\big(\hat n_{(i,p)}-1\big)-\lambda\!\!\sum_{p,q} c(p-q)\,\hat n_p\hat n_q \]
(Q)

a Bose–Hubbard model on \(\mathbb{Z}_{12}\) with a long-range interaction whose coupling is the consonance kernel. The unison penalty \(U\) is the Hubbard repulsion; \(J\) is new — a coherent semitone hop with no classical counterpart. In the one-token-per-voice sector this is

\[ \hat H_Q=-J\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big(S_i+S_i^\dagger\big)+\hat V,\qquad S_i:\ |n\rangle\mapsto|n+e_i\rangle, \]

with \(\hat V\) diagonal in the chord basis. But a closed \(\hat H_Q\) would be a generalisation of the wrong thing: it throws away the stochasticity that made this Xenakis in the first place. Keep it, by promoting each classical rate to a Lindblad jump operator:

\[ L_\tau=\sum_n \sqrt{w_i^{s}(n)}\ \big|\,n+se_i\,\big\rangle\big\langle\, n\,\big|,\qquad L_\tau^\dagger L_\tau=\sum_n w_i^s(n)\,|n\rangle\langle n| \]
(L)
\[ \boxed{\;\frac{d\rho}{dt}=-i\big[\hat H_Q,\rho\big]+\sum_\tau\Big(L_\tau\rho L_\tau^\dagger-\tfrac12\big\{L_\tau^\dagger L_\tau,\rho\big\}\Big)+\kappa\sum_n\Big(\Pi_n\rho\,\Pi_n-\tfrac12\{\Pi_n,\rho\}\Big)\;} \]

with \(\Pi_n=|n\rangle\langle n|\). This is the quantum Xenakis equation. Three things make it the right generalisation rather than a different model wearing the name:

  • It contains the original exactly. Set \(J=0\): the commutator dies, the jumps map diagonals to diagonals, and \(\rho_{nn}\) obeys (M) identically — numerically to \(5.6\times10^{-17}\), not approximately.
  • Decoherence is the classical limit. For any \(J\), sending \(\kappa\to\infty\) drives the populations back onto (M). Eliminating the coherences adiabatically gives the extra Zeno-suppressed hopping rate \(W_{m\leftarrow n}=2J^2\kappa/\big(\kappa^2+[V_m-V_n]^2\big)\), which vanishes as \(1/\kappa\) — the measured collapse follows that law.
  • The dictionary is Baez–Biamonte's own. \(e^{tH}\) stochastic becomes \(e^{t\mathcal L}\) completely positive; \(\langle-|H=0\) becomes \(\operatorname{tr}\mathcal L(\rho)=0\); the infinitesimal-stochastic \(H\) is the restriction of \(\mathcal L\) to the diagonal subalgebra.

What is actually new

Interference. Two voice-leading paths from one chord to another now carry amplitudes that can cancel. Coherent transport is ballistic where the Markov chain is diffusive: starting from C–E–G, the r.m.s. distance travelled in chord space grows as \(t^{0.98}\) against the chain's \(t^{0.50}\), measured on the same lattice with the same \(V\). The quantum ensemble reaches chords the classical one has not yet found — and, at particular times, has near-zero amplitude on chords the classical walk visits constantly.

Entangled voices. \(\hat H_Q\) does not factorise across voices, so the generic state is not "voice 1 on some pitch, voice 2 on some pitch". It is a chord with no definite pitch content until measured — a superposition of C major and F♯ major that is not a mixture of the two, and whose voices are correlated in a way no probability distribution over chords can reproduce.

Measurement as a compositional parameter. A score is a sequence of definite chords, so writing one down means measuring. Evolve for an interval \(\tau\), sample \(n\sim|\langle n|\psi\rangle|^2\), collapse, repeat. Then \(\tau\) is a knob with no classical analogue: as \(\tau\to0\) the quantum Zeno effect freezes the chord entirely, and as \(\tau\) grows the score explores further per event than any Markov chain with the same rates. The composer's choice of how often to look becomes a parameter of the piece.

On the sources. The stochastic-mechanics formalism — stochastic states, the sum covector, infinitesimal stochastic Hamiltonians, the Petri-net Hamiltonian (BB) and its companion rate equation — follows John Baez and Jacob Biamonte, Quantum Techniques for Stochastic Mechanics. The music is Iannis Xenakis's: the sound graph and the ruled glissando surfaces of Metastaseis, the Maxwell–Boltzmann pitch cloud of Pithoprakta, and the screen-to-screen Markov chains of Analogique A/B, all set out in Formalized Music. The tension operator, the quantum lift and every number on this page are constructions of this write-up, not of either source.
